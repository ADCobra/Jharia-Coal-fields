              How Jharia Fire Watch works

              Coal seams under Jharia have burned underground since 1916. The fires hollow out the ground, and roads above them can sink. Fire Watch checks every satellite photo and protects the roads.

              1. Satellite photo. Landsat 8 and 9 photograph Jharia every 8 days, including a heat (thermal) image.
              2. Hot ground found. OpenCV lines the photo up with earlier ones and finds ground hotter than it should be. Sun-heated coal and dumps are filtered out.
              3. Spread predicted. Each fire is followed from photo to photo, and Fire Watch predicts where it could reach in the next 48 days.
              4. Roads protected. An AI agent closes a road only when a fire is clearly close and it is confident. When unsure, it asks you. Only a person can reopen a road.

              More detail, a guided tour and answers to common questions are on the Help page.

              About the data

              Everything here comes from free, public sources. Here is what they are and where they fall short.

              Satellite photos
              Landsat 8 and 9 Collection 2 Level-2 scenes (USGS), read from Microsoft Planetary Computer. Each pass brings a surface temperature image and an ordinary colour photo.
              Heat image
              Landsat measures heat at 100 m and USGS delivers it on a 30 m grid. A fire is ground clearly hotter than its surroundings once sun-warmed coal and bare ground are accounted for.
              Roads, mines and plants
              OpenStreetMap. Roads are cut into sections of about 400 m so a closure can be precise.
              Sharper photos
              Sentinel-2 (ESA), used by the agent to double-check a fire when it can reach the archive.

              What it can't do

              • Landsat passes over Jharia at about 10:15 in the morning and sees heat at the surface, not the fire itself. A deep fire can be missed.
              • Cloud hides the ground. Fire Watch waits for a clear photo instead of guessing.
              • Coal dumps and dark ground warmed by the sun can look like fire. Anything doubtful comes to you.
              • This is a research project, not an official closure notice.
